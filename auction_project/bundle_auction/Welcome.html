The experiment consists of a series of trading rounds. You will be randomly assigned as either a Buyer or a Seller for the entire experiment. Within the group of participants in this experiment there will be an equal number of Buyers and Sellers.

⚠️ Your role as a Buyer or a Seller will be randomly reassigned at the start of each new round.

Market rules:
{{ if treatment == 'baseline' }}

You will be trading products representing two distinct types of goods, labelled as Product A and Product B. Each type of product is bought and sold in separate markets. You can choose to trade either product at any point during the round.

{{ endif }} {{ if treatment == 'single_package' }}

You will be trading packages containing a combination of two distinct goods, labelled as Product A and Product B. Each Package contains exactly 1 unit of each product (1:1 ratio).

{{ endif }} {{ if treatment == 'package_menu' }}

You will be trading packages containing a combination of two distinct goods, labelled as Product A and Product B. Each type of package is bought and sold in separate markets. You can choose to trade either package at any point during the round. Note that each package contains a different amount of each product:

  • Package 1 contains exactly {{ x_weight }} units of Product A and 1 unit of Product B ({{ x_weight }}:1 ratio).
  • Package 2 contains exactly 1 unit of Product A and {{ x_weight }} units of Product B (1:{{ x_weight }} ratio).
{{ endif }}

Your primary objective as either Buyer or Seller is to maximise your total profit.

⚠️ The products your trade are interdependent. This means that the number of units you have already bought or sold of one product directly affects your current Marginal Cost or Marginal Value for the other product. As you Buy or Sell more units, the value or cost for the next unit will change.

Trading guidelines:
  • As a Seller, you should sell units at a price higher than your Marginal Cost (MC).
  • As a Buyer, you should purchase units at a price lower than your Marginal Value (MV).
